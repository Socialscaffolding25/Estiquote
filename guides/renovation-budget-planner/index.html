A renovation budget planner you can actually use

Keep three numbers separate: the work you have priced, the items still uncertain and the amount you can afford. That makes a budget easier to update when real quotes arrive.

Start with a room-by-room brief

For each room, record what stays, what changes and what is still undecided. A kitchen refresh that keeps its layout needs a different brief from a renovation that moves walls, services and openings.

List the finish you expect and who supplies each item. “New bathroom” leaves far more room for different interpretations than a list of fixtures, tiling areas, lighting and installation requirements.

A worked budget, with assumptions visible

This fictional example demonstrates the arithmetic only. It is not an estimate of what a renovation normally costs. Assume all amounts are on the same tax-inclusive basis where tax applies.

  • Construction quote: £48,000.
  • Owner-supplied appliances excluded from that quote: £4,000.
  • Separately priced design and inspection costs: £3,000.
  • Chosen contingency: £5,000.
  • Planning budget: £60,000. A further £2,000 optional upgrade would bring it to £62,000 if selected.

If the construction quote already includes the appliances, do not add them again. If a line is unknown, label it as unresolved rather than treating the £60,000 as complete.

Choose a contingency around the unknowns

A contingency is money held for uncertainty, not an invitation to spend the full amount on upgrades. Record what is uncertain: concealed conditions, unresolved design or items that cannot yet be accurately priced.

There is no percentage that makes every project safe. Ask your project professional to help assess the risks. Estiquote displays a 15% contingency to hold alongside its planning midpoint; that is a model assumption, not a guarantee that it will cover your project.

Replace assumptions as information improves

  1. Keep the original allowance and its source in your notes.
  2. Add the dated written price when it arrives.
  3. Check scope and tax before replacing the allowance.
  4. Update the remaining contingency only after understanding the change.

GOV.UK’s VAT guide for building work explains that treatment can vary. Confirm the applicable amount with the supplier instead of assuming every line uses the same rate.

Track payments separately from the total budget

A £60,000 budget does not tell you when money will be needed. Beside the budget, note the agreed payment stages and any owner-supplied items you must buy before installation. Keep invoices and payment records with the written agreement.

Turn the budget into a better conversation

If quotes exceed what you can afford, identify changes to scope and specification and have the builders reprice them. Do not simply reduce an allowance on paper and assume the work becomes cheaper.
